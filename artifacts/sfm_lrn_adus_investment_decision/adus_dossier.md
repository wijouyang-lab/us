# ADUS — Addus HomeCare

**Decision: WATCH** (not PRIORITY_WATCH)  
**Confidence: MEDIUM**  
**Price: $122.53 (2026-11-25); market cap $2.283B; net cash $75M**

## 1. Business

Addus provides home health, hospice and personal-care services, primarily to elderly and disabled patients. Payers are largely Medicare, Medicaid and managed-care organizations. Revenue is driven by billable census/hours, reimbursement rates and acquisitions. It is a labor-intensive, reimbursement-driven business, not a product company.

## 2. Growth decomposition

Q3 2026:

- Revenue **$387.7M**, +7.0% year over year.
- Same-store revenue growth **6.9%** vs 6.6% prior-year quarter.
- Homecourt acquisition closed May 1, 2026; acquisition contribution is not separately disclosed in XBRL.
- Therefore: **organic growth ~6.9%**, acquisition/other ~0.1pp (residual), but the exact split is **UNKNOWN** because the company does not disclose it cleanly in the filing.

This is genuinely organic, not just roll-up growth — a positive distinction versus SFM and LRN.

## 3. Financials

TTM: revenue **$1.502B**, net income **$111.1M**, OCF **$152.1M**, capex **$7.8M**, FCF **$144.2M**.

FY2025: revenue $1.422B, net income $95.9M, FCF $103.8M. TTM FCF improved ~$40M, helped by higher OCF and low capex. Net cash ~$75M; debt is small ($21.9M). The balance sheet is not a constraint.

Cash flow quality: positive and less seasonal than LRN; capex is only ~0.5% of revenue. But Medicaid receivables and acquisition working capital can distort timing. The company is a serial acquirer; goodwill/intangibles should be monitored.

## 4. 8-quarter data

`quarterly_financials.csv` contains eight quarters of revenue/net income. OCF is YTD-tagged; single-quarter OCF is not consistently available from XBRL. The TTM cash-flow figures are the reliable basis.

## 5. Moat and competition

Strengths: local density, caregiver relationships, payer contracts, three service lines, demographic demand. Competitors include home-care providers, hospices, managed-care-owned networks and hospital-at-home programs. The moat is local scale and contracts, not a durable brand.

## 6. Risks

- **Medicaid/Medicare rate cuts and managed-care transition** — HIGH.
- **Labor shortages/wage inflation and caregiver retention** — HIGH.
- Acquisition integration and goodwill — MEDIUM.
- Payer concentration and audits — HIGH.
- Scaled operator but low margins; small rate changes hit earnings.
- Valuation at ~20.6x TTM P/E leaves limited room for a policy shock.

## 7. Valuation

TTM: P/E **20.6**, EV/FCF **15.3**, FCF yield **6.3%**, net cash.

Scenarios (FCF × multiple + net cash; not a target price):

| Scenario | FCF | Multiple | Implied equity | Implied price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | $110M | 13x | $1.51B | **$80.77** |
| Base | $150M | 16x | $2.48B | **$132.83** |
| Bull | $175M | 19x | $3.40B | **$182.47** |

Current $122.53 is around the base case; bear case ~34% downside. There is no clear margin of safety at 20x earnings for a Medicaid-exposed services business.

**Watch price:** below ~$100 (roughly 17x TTM P/E, ~12x EV/FCF) **and** organic growth >6% with stable rates. Not a buy order.

## 8. Catalysts / next review

- State Medicaid rate updates and managed-care contracts.
- Homecourt integration and organic growth.
- Caregiver hiring/retention and wage inflation.
- Q4 2026/Q1 2027 revenue and margin.

## 9. Verdict

Best organic growth of the three, clean balance sheet, positive FCF. But valuation and Medicaid policy risk are not compensated enough. **WATCH, MEDIUM confidence**; it is the closest to PRIORITY_WATCH, but not there yet.
