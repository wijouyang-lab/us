# LRN — Stride

**Decision: WATCH** (not PRIORITY_WATCH)  
**Confidence: LOW**  
**Price: $79.90 (2026-11-25); market cap $3.360B; net cash $129M**

## 1. Business

Stride provides online K-12 general education, career learning and adult programs. Revenue comes mainly from public/charter school contracts, often on a per-enrollment or per-pupil funding basis. The customer is the school/state program, not the individual family in most cases. This makes revenue sensitive to state authorizations, funding formulas and enrollment audits.

## 2. Growth and operations

Latest reported quarter (Q1 FY2027, ended 2026-09-30):

- Revenue **$639.4M** vs $620.9M prior-year quarter, +3.0%.
- Gross margin **37.7%** vs 39.0%.
- Operating income $84.3M vs $69.0M.
- Net income $64.9M vs $68.8M.
- YTD OCF **-$124.8M**; Q1 is seasonally cash-negative.

FY2026: revenue $2.518B, operating income $450.8M, net income $338.2M, FCF $433.2M.

**Critical data gap:** the 10-Q does not provide a clean, extractable student-enrollment count or retention rate in XBRL; the filing describes enrollment mechanics but not a numeric series. We therefore **cannot verify whether revenue growth is enrollment-driven, price/funding-driven, or mix-driven**. Any claim that growth is organic enrollment growth would be unsupported.

## 3. Cash flow

TTM: revenue $2.537B, net income $334.3M, OCF $504.8M, capex $0.3M, FCF **$504.5M**. This is unusually high FCF conversion because capex is minimal.

But the quarterly cash-flow pattern is seasonal and volatile: Q1 FY2027 OCF was **-$124.8M**. The FY/TTM FCF is real, but it is not a stable quarterly annuity. The 8-quarter table has YTD-based cash flow; we do not present those as standalone quarterly FCF.

## 4. Moat and competition

Strengths: curriculum, school relationships, online delivery scale, two decades of operating history, and a low-capex model. Competitors include school districts, other virtual/charter operators, curriculum providers and nonprofit online schools.

The moat is **contractual/regulatory**, not consumer-brand based. A state can change funding, authorization or enrollment rules; that is the central risk.

## 5. Risks

- State funding/authorization and for-profit education regulation — **HIGH**.
- Enrollment/retention data not verified — **HIGH** (information risk).
- Customer concentration in state/school contracts — **HIGH**.
- Seasonal cash flow and Q1 OCF deficit — **MEDIUM**.
- Reputation/regulatory scrutiny of virtual charter outcomes — **HIGH**.
- Low capex means FCF can look strong while growth investment is off-balance-sheet/opex — **MEDIUM**.

## 7. Catalysts / next review

- Fall enrollment counts and retention disclosures.
- State authorization/renewal decisions.
- FY2027 guidance and Q2/Q3 cash conversion.
- Any regulatory action on virtual charter funding.

## 8. Verdict

Statistically cheap with high FCF, but the key operating driver (enrollment) and state-regulatory risk are not verifiable from the data extracted. That is not enough for PRIORITY_WATCH. **WATCH, LOW confidence.**
